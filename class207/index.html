Beyond Level 80

Level 80 isn't the finish line. It's where Guild Wars 2 stops telling you one obvious direction to go and starts letting you choose your own.

Masteries Endgame Next Steps
At a Glance

You don't have to do everything

Choose

Pick something interesting

Story, maps, Masteries, Fractals, Strikes, PvP, WvW, collections, crafting, fashion, achievements, or just wandering around are all valid directions.

Build

Improve what matters to you

Level 80 progression is often about expanding your options, unlocking account features, improving gear, and learning your character.

Explore

Follow curiosity

If something looks cool, investigate it. Guild Wars 2 rewards curiosity more than following one mandatory endgame path.

Core Lesson

Level 80 gives you choices, not homework

The game can suddenly feel overwhelming at level 80 because dozens of systems, maps, currencies, achievements, and activities become relevant at the same time.

Don't read that as a giant checklist.

Think of level 80 like arriving at a train station with a dozen destinations. You don't need to board every train today. Pick one place you actually want to visit.

Part 1 // The Level 80 Shift

Your character level stops. Progress doesn't.

After level 80, progression starts spreading into other systems instead of raising your character to level 81, 82, and beyond.

Masteries

Account progression

Masteries unlock new abilities and conveniences such as movement tools, exploration features, and other account-wide benefits.

Equipment

Finish a real build

Start putting together equipment with attributes that actually match what your build is trying to do.

Experience

You get better

Your biggest upgrades eventually come from understanding your profession, encounters, maps, and game systems better.

Returning Player Note

Old good gear can still be good gear

Guild Wars 2 doesn't continually raise the maximum character level every expansion and force everyone to replace their equipment with a brand-new higher-level set.

If you're returning with level-80 gear, don't immediately throw it away. First check its rarity, attributes, upgrades, and whether those stats still make sense for the build you want to play.

Part 2 // Pick a Direction

What sounds fun right now?

Story & Exploration

Continue the story, explore expansion maps, unlock Masteries, and discover new parts of Tyria.

Open World

Join meta events, world bosses, map achievements, collections, and large groups of players.

Fractals

Five-player instanced content that grows into a deeper endgame progression system.

Strikes & Raids

Organized group encounters where mechanics, builds, support, and coordination become more important.

PvP & WvW

Fight other players in structured matches or large-scale World versus World battles.

Collections & Fashion

Chase skins, achievements, mounts, titles, Legendary equipment, decorations, or whatever gives your account personality.

Part 3 // Masteries

Experience keeps doing something after level 80

Masteries are one of Guild Wars 2's major forms of horizontal progression.

Instead of simply making your character a higher level, Masteries can unlock new ways to move through maps, interact with the world, use account features, or access new conveniences.

Good Starting Habit

Whenever you're playing in a region with Masteries available, make sure you're training something instead of letting the system sit forgotten.

Long-Term Goals

Legendary gear is convenience, not permission to play

Legendary equipment is a major long-term goal for many players because of its flexibility and account convenience.

But don't look at another player's full Legendary Armory and assume you need that before joining the rest of the game.

Start with sensible equipment for the content you're doing. Legendary gear can become a project later if that kind of progression sounds fun to you.

Veteran Shortcuts

Little things worth knowing

/wiki

Look things up quickly

Use the in-game wiki command when an item, achievement, currency, or mechanic makes absolutely no sense.

Looking For Group

Find people already doing it

The LFG panel is worth checking when you're looking for groups, squads, events, Fractals, Strikes, and other activities.

Deposit Materials

Clear the bags

Keep using Deposit All Materials as part of your regular inventory cleanup.

Chat Links

Show, don't describe

Link waypoints, items, and other game information when asking for help.

Action Camera

Optional control style

Action Camera changes how aiming and camera control feel. Try it if normal mouse controls aren't comfortable for you.

Settings

Make the game fit you

Revisit your keybinds, camera settings, UI options, and graphics as you learn what actually feels comfortable.

Ask for Help

You don't have to solve Tyria alone

If you're stuck on an event, Hero Point, achievement, or enemy, asking nearby players can be faster than fighting the problem alone for twenty minutes.

Use map chat, link the nearest waypoint or location, and explain what you're trying to do.

Example

“Anyone able to help with this Hero Point?” plus a waypoint or location link is usually much more useful than simply typing “help.”

Returning Player Reset

Haven't played in years?

Don't start by opening every achievement panel and trying to remember your entire account.

1

Read your character

Check your profession, weapons, traits, skills, equipment, and stat combinations.

2

Find an easy fight

Spend a little time in the open world remembering how your movement and combat feel.

3

Pick one goal

Continue a story chapter, unlock a Mastery, join an event, try a Fractal, or work on one collection.

Veteran Field Note

The best next step is the one you'll actually enjoy

There will always be a more efficient farm, a better gold-per-hour route, another achievement, or another piece of gear somebody says you “should” have.

Efficiency is a tool. It doesn't need to become the reason you play.

Find something that gives your account a direction, then let the rest of the systems become useful when you actually need them.

Field Training Complete

You know enough to start choosing for yourself

The 100-series helped you navigate the game. The 200-series gave you the language behind combat, groups, gear, and progression.

The next step is learning how all of those systems connect inside your own character.
