Advanced Guide // 202

Groups & Chat

Playing with other people doesn't need to be complicated. Find the group, stay near them, communicate when you need to, and help out.

Parties Squads Chat
At a Glance

What matters most

Find the Group

Look for the tag

Commander and mentor-style map icons give players a clear place to gather during events and open-world activities.

Stay Nearby

Don't wander too far

Healing, boons, cleanses, and other support often have limited range. Being near your group makes everybody's job easier.

Communicate

Links save a lot of typing

You can link items, locations, skills, and other information directly into chat instead of trying to describe them.

Core Lesson

You don't need to know everything to play with people

Group play can look intimidating when twenty people are moving at once and chat is full of abbreviations.

Most of the time, you can start with something much simpler: find the group, follow the group, and pay attention when they stop or move.

Think of a commander tag like someone holding a flashlight during a night walk. They don't have to explain the entire forest. They're giving everyone a clear place to gather.

Part 1 // Groups

Party, squad, or just people nearby?

Guild Wars 2 lets players work together pretty naturally. You don't always need a formal group to help with an event, but parties and squads make coordination easier.

Nearby Players

Just show up

For many open-world events, you can simply arrive and help. Everyone contributes toward the same objective.

Party

Small group

Parties are useful when a handful of players want to stay together for things like exploration, story, dungeons, or Fractals.

Squad

Larger group

Squads are built for larger organized activities and can be split into smaller subgroups for support and coordination.

Tags & Map Icons

A visible place to gather

Tags and map icons make a player easier to spot. During events, players often use them as a rally point.

Following

Watch where the tag goes

If you're unsure where an event is moving next, staying near the visible tag is usually a good starting point.

Leading

You don't need to be an encyclopedia

A useful leader can simply give people a clear location, direction, or objective. Knowing every mechanic in the game isn't required to help people organize.

Group Positioning

Why does everyone keep standing on top of each other?

Being close together makes it easier for support skills to reach the whole group.

Think of it like sharing one big umbrella. If you're twenty feet away, the healing, boons, barrier, or cleanses meant for the group may never reach you.

Of course, some mechanics tell you to spread out. When that happens, follow the mechanic first.

Part 2 // Chat

Link it instead of describing it

Guild Wars 2 can place clickable game information directly into chat. This works for many things like items, locations, skills, and equipment.

Ctrl + Click

Send it immediately

Use this when you just want to post the link directly into the active chat channel.

Shift + Click

Add it to your message

Use this when you want the link placed in your chat box so you can type something around it before sending.

Everyday Chat Tricks

A few things that save time

Map Location

Show people where you mean

Link a waypoint or map location instead of trying to explain which hill, cave, or corner of the map you're standing in.

Items & Gear

Ask about the actual item

If you don't know what something is, link it directly so another player can see exactly what you're talking about.

“Ping Gear”

They're asking for a link

If someone asks you to ping a piece of gear, they usually just want you to link that equipment into chat so they can inspect it.

/wiki

One of the best commands in the game

The /wiki command can open the Guild Wars 2 Wiki directly from chat.

Look Up an Item

Type /wiki, add an item link to the message, and send it to open that item's wiki page.

Event Timers

Type /wiki et to open the event timer page for scheduled world events.

Veteran Field Note

Asking a question is faster than pretending you know

Guild Wars 2 has been around for a long time. People throw around shorthand like everyone was born knowing it.

If someone says “stack,” “CC,” “ping,” or names a mechanic you don't know, asking what they mean is usually easier than trying to fake your way through the next five minutes.

Try It in Tyria

Get comfortable using chat

No checklist. Just try these once so you know where the tools are.

Link

Shift-click an item or map location into chat and add a few words before sending it.

Lookup

Use /wiki on an item you don't recognize and see where it takes you.

Follow

Join a busy open-world event and practice staying near the tag until you understand where the group is moving.
