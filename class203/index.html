Crafting

Crafting turns the materials you've been collecting into gear, food, weapons, upgrades, and plenty of other useful things.

Disciplines Recipes Materials
At a Glance

What crafting actually does

Materials

Raw pieces

Wood, ore, cloth, leather, trophies, and other materials are the ingredients you build with.

Recipes

Instructions

Recipes tell a crafting discipline which ingredients combine into a finished item.

Discipline

Your trade

Each discipline specializes in certain kinds of armor, weapons, food, trinkets, or other crafted items.

Core Lesson

You don't need to become a crafter right away

Crafting is useful, but it isn't something a new player has to master before they can enjoy the rest of Guild Wars 2.

Think of it like a workshop. You collect parts while playing, store them until you need them, then come back when you have something specific you want to build.

If you're not sure what to craft yet, keeping your materials and learning the basic interface is enough.

Part 1 // Disciplines

Pick a trade that makes something useful to you

A character normally keeps two crafting disciplines active at once. You can change disciplines later without losing the progress you've already earned in them.

Armorsmith

Heavy armor and related metal equipment.

Leatherworker

Medium armor, leather gear, and bags.

Tailor

Light armor, cloth equipment, and bags.

Weaponsmith

Many melee weapons, shields, and metal weapon components.

Huntsman

Bows, guns, torches, warhorns, and other ranged equipment.

Artificer

Magical weapons such as staves, scepters, and foci.

Chef

Food, ingredients, and consumables that provide useful bonuses.

Jeweler

Rings, accessories, amulets, and other trinket-related items.

Scribe

Guild-related items, decorations, and specialized guild crafting.

Handiworker

Homestead decorations and other housing-related crafting.

Where Should I Start?

Match a discipline to something you use

One easy starting point is your armor weight. Heavy characters can look at Armorsmith, medium characters at Leatherworker, and light characters at Tailor.

Your second discipline can match a weapon type you like, or you can pick something completely different like Chef. There's no permanent mistake here.

Part 2 // The Crafting Bench

The interface is less scary than it looks

Once you open a crafting station for your discipline, most of what you need lives in a few basic areas.

Production

Make known recipes

This is where you craft items and components from recipes your character already knows.

Discovery

Learn new combinations

Place compatible ingredients together to discover certain new recipes.

Storage

Your materials are nearby

Crafting stations give you convenient access to your Bank and Material Storage while you work.

Discovery

Some recipes are learned by experimenting

The Discovery tab lets you combine compatible crafting components. When you've found a valid combination, you can create the item and permanently add that recipe to your known list.

You don't have to randomly throw every material you own into the window. Think of Discovery as another tool for learning recipes, not a guessing game you need to solve from scratch.

Material Tiers

Better recipes need better materials

Materials roughly move upward in tiers as crafting gets more advanced. Early recipes use things like copper, jute, rawhide, and green wood. Higher crafting ranges start asking for materials like mithril, silk, elder wood, orichalcum, gossamer, and ancient wood.

Early

Copper, iron, jute, wool, rawhide, thin leather, and lower-tier wood.

Mid

Steel, darksteel, cotton, linen, coarse leather, rugged leather, and stronger wood.

High

Mithril, orichalcum, silk, gossamer, thick or hardened leather, elder wood, and ancient wood.

Easy Version

If a recipe asks for materials you've never seen before, you're probably looking at something meant for a later crafting range.

Higher-Level Crafting

Some projects take more than materials

As you get into more advanced crafting, you'll start seeing refined materials, special recipes, expensive components, and items that can only be produced on a limited schedule.

That's normal. Endgame crafting is often less like making a sword from a few ore pieces and more like slowly assembling a project.

Don't Rush It

If you run into a daily limit or a recipe with a long shopping list, you probably haven't done anything wrong. Some higher-end crafting is intentionally built around longer-term progress.

Veteran Field Note

Don't craft something just because you can

Materials have value. Before spending a pile of them on equipment, check whether crafting is actually the easiest or cheapest way to get what you're after.

Sometimes crafting is the answer. Sometimes the Trading Post, achievements, currencies, or another reward path makes more sense.

Try It in Tyria

Visit a crafting station

You don't need to level a discipline today. Just get familiar with what the workshop looks like.

Browse

Talk to a crafting trainer and look through the disciplines available to you.

Open

Visit a crafting station and look through Production, Discovery, and your stored materials.

Make

If you already have the materials, craft one simple component or recipe just to see how the process works.
