Advanced Guide // 305

Fractals of the Mists

Fractals are short five-player adventures that grow harder as you climb. Start small, learn the mechanics, and add new tools as the Mists start pushing back.

5 Players Scales 1–100 Agony Daily Content
At a Glance

What makes Fractals different?

Short

One contained adventure

Each fractal has its own setting, enemies, mechanics, and boss encounters.

Scaling

The same system gets harder

Difficulty increases through scales 1–100, adding stronger enemies, Agony, and eventually Mistlock Instabilities.

Repeatable

Learn through repetition

Daily Fractals give you a reason to revisit encounters and become more comfortable with them over time.

Core Lesson

Tier 1 is where you learn the language

Your first Fractals aren't supposed to be a test of whether you're already an expert.

Tier 1 gives you room to learn how five-player groups move, where mechanics happen, what the bosses do, and which parts of your build are useful.

Think of the early scales like learning a course before somebody starts adding rain, darkness, and obstacles.

Part 1 // Fractal Basics

The Mistlock Observatory is your staging area

Fractals of the Mists are accessed through the Mistlock Observatory. This is the lobby where your party gathers before starting a run.

Party

Up to five players

Fractals are designed around small-group play rather than the large squads used for open-world metas.

Scale

Choose the difficulty

The selected scale determines which fractal you enter and how difficult the run will be.

Personal Level

Your account progresses

Completing Fractals at the appropriate scale raises your account's Personal Fractal Level over time.

Part 2 // Difficulty Tiers

Four tiers, one progression path

Tier 1

1–25

Learn encounters, basic group movement, bosses, and Fractal mechanics.

Tier 2

26–50

Agony matters more and one Mistlock Instability changes the rules of the run.

Tier 3

51–75

Two instabilities are active and mistakes become more expensive.

Tier 4

76–100

Three instabilities, higher incoming pressure, and greater expectations for group preparation.

First Fractal?

Start in Tier 1

You don't need to leap immediately into the highest scale your party can enter.

T1 exists for learning. Tell the group you're new, watch what people do, and ask questions when something doesn't make sense.

Knowing the encounter will eventually matter more than pretending you already know it.

Part 3 // Agony

The mechanic that gates higher scales

Starting at scale 20, certain attacks can apply Agony.

Agony deals heavy damage based on your maximum health and reduces incoming healing. The higher the Fractal scale, the more Agony Resistance you need to reduce its damage.

Agony Resistance

Fractal-specific defense

AR helps against Agony. It doesn't make you generally tougher against every normal attack.

Infusions

Where AR usually comes from

Agony Infusions are placed into infusion slots found on Ascended and Legendary equipment.

Scale 100

Up to 150 AR

The highest standard scale calls for roughly 150 Agony Resistance.

Don't Overgear Early

You don't need 150 AR for Tier 1

Agony Resistance is something you build as you climb.

Early T1 scales don't use Agony at all. By scale 25, the suggested amount is only around 17 AR.

Gear for the part of the ladder you're actually climbing instead of treating 150 AR like an entrance fee for your first Fractal.

Part 4 // Daily Fractals

The daily list gives your group a destination

Each day, three Fractals are selected as Daily Fractals across the difficulty tiers.

Recommended Fractals also provide additional targets in the lower three tiers.

You don't need to memorize the rotation. Check the current Daily achievements or Fractal interface when you're ready to run.

Check

Today's targets

See which Fractals are currently giving Daily or Recommended rewards.

Choose

Your tier

Run the difficulty that matches your Personal Fractal Level, equipment, AR, and comfort.

Prepare

Read before entering

At higher tiers, check the active instabilities and adjust your utility skills if needed.

Part 5 // Five-Player Combat

Stay close enough to help each other

Fractals start teaching habits that become important throughout organized Guild Wars 2 combat.

Stay With the Group

Boons, healing, revives, and other support are easier when the party isn't scattered across the room.

Handle Mechanics

Damage matters less when the entire group is repeatedly getting hit by avoidable attacks.

Revive

Help downed teammates when it's safe. Five players working together are stronger than four players ignoring the floor.

Bring CC

When a Defiance Bar appears, crowd control can interrupt the encounter and create an opening.

Cleanse Conditions

Burning, Bleeding, Poison, Torment, and other conditions can create heavy pressure if nobody removes them.

Adapt

Swap utility skills when the encounter needs something your normal setup doesn't provide.

Fractal Toolbox

Useful words you'll hear

You already learned most of these systems elsewhere in Terminal 77-B. Fractals are where you start using them together.

CC

Daze, stun, knockback, pull, fear, and other control effects damage active Defiance Bars.

Cleanse

Removes harmful conditions from yourself or allies.

Stability

Helps prevent many crowd-control effects such as knockbacks, launches, pulls, and fears.

Boon Strip

Removes helpful boons from enemies when those boons are making them harder to fight.

Projectile Defense

Blocks, absorbs, or reflects projectiles when the encounter allows it.

Boons

Might, Fury, Quickness, Alacrity, Protection, Stability, and other boons help the group perform more effectively.

Sustain

Healing, Barrier, defensive boons, and positioning keep pressure manageable.

Movement

Sometimes the best defensive skill is simply getting out of the attack before it lands.

Build Theory in Practice

Your utility bar isn't carved in stone

One of the best habits Fractals can teach you is changing a utility skill because the encounter needs something different.

Need more crowd control? Bring it. Getting buried under conditions? Bring cleanse. Constant knockbacks? Look for Stability.

A build is a toolbox, not a museum display.

Part 6 // Mistlock Instabilities

Higher tiers start changing the rules

Mistlock Instabilities are additional modifiers that change how a Fractal behaves.

Tier 2 introduces one instability. Tier 3 uses two. Tier 4 can use three.

You do not need to memorize every instability before starting Fractals. Learn to read the active ones and identify what they want you to change.

More Conditions?

Think about condition cleanse and defensive positioning.

Enemy Boons?

Consider boon removal or boon corruption.

Movement Hazard?

Give the group room and pay closer attention to positioning.

More Damage?

Respect mechanics sooner instead of trying to absorb every hit.

Common Examples

Read the problem, then bring the answer

Afflicted

Enemies apply additional damaging conditions.

Think: Condition Cleanse
No Pain, No Gain

Enemies gain boons that can make them harder to defeat.

Think: Boon Removal
Social Awkwardness

Players can push each other when standing too close.

Think: Positioning
Flux Bomb

A damaging field periodically appears around a player.

Think: Move Away From Group
Field Note

Treat instabilities like weather

You don't argue with the rain.

You see that it's raining and bring a jacket.

Fractal instabilities work the same way. Identify what changed, bring the right tool, and keep moving.

Part 7 // Defeat & Recovery

Going down doesn't end the run

Fractals do not use normal open-world waypoints inside the instance. The party relies on reviving teammates and Fractal checkpoints.

If somebody goes down, decide whether it's safe to help them instead of automatically abandoning the fight.

And if you're the person who went down, pay attention to why. That information is often more useful than the death itself.

New Player Etiquette

“I'm new” is useful information

If you haven't done a Fractal before, saying so gives the party a chance to explain mechanics instead of assuming you already know them.

You don't need a speech. Something as simple as “First time here, anything important I should know?” does the job.

Simple Fractal Routine

Before the portal opens

1

Check the selected scale

Make sure the difficulty matches your Personal Fractal Level, gear, and Agony Resistance.

2

Read the instabilities

If you're in T2 or higher, see what modifiers are active.

3

Check your utilities

Decide whether you need extra CC, cleanse, Stability, boon removal, projectile defense, or another tool.

4

Enter together

Give the party time to arrive and prepare before activating the run.

First Goal

Don't chase perfect damage yet

On your first few runs, focus on staying with the party and learning what the encounter is asking you to do.

Once the mechanics become familiar, you'll naturally spend less time reacting late and more time attacking.

Survival and understanding create the space where good damage happens.

Try It in Tyria

Run one Tier 1 Fractal

Don't worry about clearing an entire progression ladder. Learn one instance.

Watch

Notice where the group moves and what changes when a boss starts a mechanic.

Ask

If something doesn't make sense, ask the party what happened.

Remember

After the run, name one mechanic or utility tool you understand better than you did before.

Veteran Field Note

Preparation beats stubbornness

If a run keeps going badly, don't automatically assume the answer is more damage.

Maybe the group needs more crowd control. Maybe conditions are overwhelming everyone. Maybe an instability is being ignored. Maybe one mechanic simply hasn't clicked yet.

Fractals reward players who can diagnose the problem and change one thing on purpose.

Lesson Complete

Climb when you're ready

Fractals aren't one giant difficulty spike. They're a staircase.

Learn the fights in Tier 1. Add Agony Resistance as you need it. Start reading instabilities in Tier 2. Keep improving your build and group awareness as you climb.

You don't need to stand at scale 100 to belong in the Mists.
