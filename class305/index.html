Module 01

Understanding Fractals

Fractals of the Mists are short, repeatable five-player dungeons. Each fractal has its own map, story, enemies, and mechanics. Your party chooses a difficulty scale from 1 to 100 before entering.

Personal Fractal Level

Complete a fractal at your current personal level or higher to increase your account-wide personal fractal level by one.

Difficulty Tiers

T1 covers scales 1-25, T2 covers 26-50, T3 covers 51-75, and T4 covers 76-100.

Mistlock Observatory

The lobby contains the scale selector, vendors, fractal potions, infusions, training tools, and the portal used to begin a run.

GENERAL RULE:

There are no normal waypoints inside a fractal. Defeated players must be revived or wait until the party leaves combat before returning to the latest checkpoint.

Module 02

Agony, Resistance, and Tier Scaling

Agony begins at scale 20. It reduces incoming healing and deals a percentage of your maximum health as repeated damage. Agony Resistance, usually added through infusions in Ascended or Legendary equipment, reduces that damage.

Agony Resistance

AR does not make you generally tougher. It specifically reduces Agony damage. Check the selected scale before entering so your resistance meets the requirement.

Mistlock Instabilities

Instabilities are rotating modifiers that change how enemies or players behave. Read them before the run because the same fractal can feel very different from one day to the next.

Module 04

Fractal Combat Fundamentals

Fractals use a lot of shorthand. These terms describe the tools that keep a group alive and make difficult encounters manageable.

Boons

Boons are positive effects. Might increases damage, Fury raises critical chance, Protection reduces damage, Quickness speeds up skill use, Alacrity shortens recharge time, and Stability prevents many control effects.

Boon Strip

Boon strip removes positive effects from enemies. If an enemy gains Protection, removing it restores your party's normal damage. It is especially useful with the No Pain, No Gain instability.

Boon Corruption

Boon corruption removes an enemy boon and replaces it with a harmful condition. It does two jobs at once: the enemy loses a benefit and gains a weakness.

Conditions

Conditions are harmful effects such as Burning, Bleeding, Poison, Chill, Cripple, Immobilize, Fear, Torment, and Confusion. Some deal damage while others limit movement or skill use.

Condition Cleanse

A condition cleanse removes harmful conditions from you or your allies. If your health is falling quickly, check whether Burning, Poison, Bleeding, or another condition is active.

Crowd Control (CC)

Crowd control disables or moves enemies. Daze, Stun, Knockback, Pull, Launch, Fear, Float, and Knockdown all count as CC and damage a boss's blue defiance bar.

Defiance Bar

The blue bar beneath a boss is its defiance bar, often called a breakbar. When it appears, use CC. Breaking it interrupts dangerous attacks and usually creates a safer damage window.

Projectile Defense

Projectile block stops arrows, bullets, and magical shots. Projectile absorb removes them. Projectile reflect sends them back at the attacker and can turn enemy damage into your own.

Stability

Stability prevents many knockbacks, pulls, launches, knockdowns, fears, and other control effects. It does not reduce damage. Think of it as keeping your feet planted.

Adapt Your Build

A single build will not solve every encounter. Swap utility skills when the group needs more cleanse, Stability, boon strip, projectile defense, or CC.

FIELD NOTE:

Damage matters, but preparation is the sharper blade. A party that brings the correct tools will often outperform a party that only brings higher numbers.

Pre-Run Checklist

  • Check the Scale: Confirm the selected fractal level and required Agony Resistance.
  • Read Instabilities: Review the active modifiers before entering.
