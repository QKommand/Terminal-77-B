Build Theory

A build isn't just a list of equipment. It's a collection of choices working toward the way you want your character to fight.

Attributes Damage Survivability Support
Build Philosophy

Build for the player behind the keyboard

Crafting a build is a little like setting up a vehicle. The fastest race car in the world isn't automatically the best vehicle for every road or every driver.

Your weapons, traits, skills, attributes, upgrades, and equipment should support what you're actually trying to accomplish.

A build that produces huge numbers on paper isn't very useful if you're uncomfortable playing it, constantly getting defeated, or don't understand why its pieces were chosen.

The Foundation

What makes a build?

01 // Weapons

What can I do?

Your weapon determines a major part of your skill bar and heavily influences how your character attacks, supports, controls, or moves.

02 // Traits

How does it behave?

Traits modify your profession and help specialize the character toward particular mechanics and roles.

03 // Skills

What tools do I carry?

Healing, utility, and elite skills give you additional tools for survival, damage, movement, control, support, and mechanics.

04 // Attributes

What gets emphasized?

Your equipment attributes determine which parts of the build receive the most statistical support.

Core Idea

Stats don't create the build by themselves

Think of attributes as fuel.

Putting Condition Damage on a character doesn't automatically create a condition build. Your weapons, skills, and traits also need to actually produce damaging conditions.

Good build theory starts by understanding what your character already does, then choosing attributes that strengthen it.

Part 1 // Direct Damage

Power, Precision & Ferocity

Direct strike damage happens when an attack hits and immediately deals damage.

Power

Stronger strikes

Power increases the damage of your direct attacks.

Precision

More critical hits

Precision increases your critical-hit chance.

Ferocity

Stronger critical hits

Ferocity increases your critical-hit damage.

Think About It Like This

Power makes the punch stronger. Precision helps the punch become a critical hit. Ferocity makes that critical hit hurt more.

Part 2 // Condition Damage

Condition Damage & Expertise

Some builds focus heavily on damaging conditions such as Bleeding, Burning, Poison, Torment, and Confusion instead of relying mainly on direct strikes.

Condition Damage

Stronger damaging conditions

Condition Damage increases the damage dealt by damaging conditions.

Expertise

Longer conditions

Expertise increases Condition Duration, allowing conditions to remain on targets longer.

Think About It Like This

Condition Damage strengthens the effect. Expertise helps keep the effect running longer.

Part 3 // Survivability

Vitality & Toughness

Offensive attributes aren't the only option. Defensive attributes can give you more room to learn, recover, and survive.

Vitality

Larger health pool

Vitality increases your maximum health, giving you a larger cushion before you're defeated.

Toughness

More armor

Toughness increases Armor, helping reduce incoming strike damage.

Important

More defense doesn't replace dodging, positioning, healing, or understanding mechanics. It simply gives you another layer of protection.

Part 4 // Support

Concentration & Healing Power

Support builds can strengthen allies through healing, boons, defensive tools, utility, and other forms of assistance.

Concentration

Longer boons

Concentration increases Boon Duration, helping the boons you provide remain active longer.

Healing Power

Stronger healing

Healing Power improves healing from abilities that scale with the attribute.

Part 5 // Attribute Combinations

Gear names are shorthand for groups of stats

At level 80, equipment commonly comes with named attribute combinations. You'll hear players refer to these names constantly when discussing builds.

Berserker's

Power, Precision, and Ferocity.

Commonly associated with direct strike-damage builds.

Marauder's

Power, Precision, Vitality, and Ferocity.

Adds Vitality while retaining a direct-damage focus.

Viper's

Power, Condition Damage, Precision, and Expertise.

Commonly associated with offensive condition builds.

Diviner's

Power, Concentration, Precision, and Ferocity.

Combines direct-damage attributes with Boon Duration.

Dragon's

Power, Ferocity, Precision, and Vitality.

Combines direct-damage attributes with additional health.

And many more

These are examples, not the complete equipment system.

Learn what the attributes mean first. The names become much easier to understand afterward.

Part 6 // The Build Loop

Build. Test. Adjust.

You don't need to understand every profession interaction before changing your build.

1

Decide what you're trying to do

Damage? Conditions? Survival? Support? A mixture?

2

Read your skills and traits

Look for repeated ideas. Critical hits? Burning? Boons? Healing? Blocks? Barrier? Those patterns tell you what the build is already trying to do.

3

Choose attributes that support it

Don't choose stats because the name sounds powerful. Choose them because you understand what they're contributing.

4

Go fight something

A build isn't finished because a website says it is. Test how it actually feels when you're moving, dodging, attacking, and reacting.

5

Adjust the weak point

If you're dying constantly, ask why. If enemies take forever to die, ask why. Change something with a reason behind it.

Veteran Field Note

Build guides are maps, not commandments

Community builds are incredibly useful. They show you combinations that experienced players have already tested and refined.

But copying a build is the beginning of understanding it, not the end.

Ask why the build chose its weapon, traits, utilities, and attributes. Once you understand those decisions, you can make better decisions of your own.

Lesson Complete

Don't memorize builds. Learn the relationships.

Power means more once you understand strike damage. Expertise means more once you understand conditions. Concentration means more once you understand boons.

That's build theory: understanding how the pieces talk to each other.
